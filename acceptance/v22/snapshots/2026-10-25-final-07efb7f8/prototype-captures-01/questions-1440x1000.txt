N06 · High-speed links

On very high-speed links, transmission time shrinks but propagation does not, so latency increasingly dominates small transfers.

Used by D4, DBP-10, DBP-11, DBP-12

N01 · What the product measures

The delay × bandwidth product describes how many bits a link holds while the first bit is still travelling from sender to receiver.

Used by D2, DBP-05, DBP-06

N02 · Computing the product

Multiply one-way latency (seconds) by bandwidth (bits per second) to get bits. Example: 50 ms × 45 Mbps = 2.25 × 10^6 bits, about 280 KB.

Used by D1, DBP-01, DBP-02, DBP-03

N03 · The pipe picture

Picture the channel as a hollow pipe: latency is the pipe’s length, bandwidth is its diameter, and the delay × bandwidth product is its volume.

Used by D2, DBP-04, DBP-05, DBP-06

N04 · A worked example

A 10 Mbps link with 20 ms one-way latency holds 200,000 bits (25,000 bytes) in transit.

Used by D1, DBP-03

N05 · Keeping the pipe full

A sender may need to send up to RTT × bandwidth bits before it hears anything back; that amount keeps the pipe full until the first reply arrives.

Used by D3, DBP-07, DBP-08, DBP-09
