Compare two single optical-fiber links. Link A is 200 km long and link B is 600 km long. Each sends the same 1,000-byte packet at 8 Mbps, and propagation speed is 200,000,000 m/s on both. Ignore queuing and processing. For each link, find transmission delay and propagation delay. Which delay changes between the links, and why?
On A, transmission and propagation are each 1 ms. On B, both are 3 ms, because transmitting across three times the distance takes three times as long.

On a 600 km optical-fiber link, the rate is 8 Mbps and propagation speed is 200,000,000 m/s. Compare sending a 1,000-byte packet with sending a 2,000-byte packet. Only packet size changes. What are transmission delay and propagation delay in each case? Explain which quantity changes.
Transmission is 1 ms for 1,000 bytes and 2 ms for 2,000 bytes. Propagation is 3 ms for both. More bits take longer to put onto the link, but the distance and propagation speed stay the same.

TEACHER-ASSIGNED REVIEW

Synthetic curated teacher-reviewed activity

Explain transmission delay as the time needed to place a packet's bits onto the link at its given rate.

Try it first

Sketch a packet being placed onto a link. Annotate which parts of the transfer depend on packet size, link bandwidth, and physical distance. Explain the distinction in your own words.

A sender replaces a short message with a longer message on the same link. Predict how the time needed to transmit the message changes, and explain what role bandwidth plays.

Sources: P07
Explanation

We often think of latency as having three components. First, there is
the speed-of-light propagation delay. This delay occurs because nothing,
including a bit on a wire, can travel faster than the speed of light. If
you know the distance between two points, you can calculate the
speed-of-light latency, although you have to be careful because light
travels across different media at different speeds: It travels at
3.0 × 10\ :sup:`8` m/s in a vacuum, 2.3 × 10\ :sup:`8` m/s
in a copper cable, and 2.0 × 10\ :sup:`8` m/s in an optical fiber.
Second, there is the amount of time it takes to transmit a unit of data.
This is a function of the network bandwidth and the size of the packet in
which the data is carried. Third, there may be queuing delays inside the
network, since packet switches generally need to store packets for some
time before forwarding them on an outbound link. So, we could define the

Check your understanding

Compare sending an identical packet on a long high bandwidth link and a short low bandwidth link. Which link needs more time to put the packet onto the link? Explain what information determines this transmission delay.
