Questions behind these answers

First question
NP-TX-01

A single cable becomes twice as long. Packet size, link transmission rate, and propagation speed stay unchanged. What happens to transmission delay and propagation delay? Explain why.

Follow-up question
NP-PROP-03

A 1,000-byte packet and a 2,000-byte packet are sent separately over the same single cable at the same transmission rate. Cable length and propagation speed are fixed. Compare their propagation delays and their transmission delays.

Follow-up question
NP-PROP-02

Two links have the same cable length and the same transmission rate. The same packet is sent over each. Link B’s effective signal propagation speed is twice Link A’s. How do propagation delay and transmission delay on B compare with A?

Reading on Transmission delay

P05 · Bandwidth of a single link

While you can talk about the bandwidth of the network as a whole, sometimes you want to be more precise, focusing, for example, on the bandwidth of a single physical link or of a logical process-to-process channel.

P07 · Three components of latency

We often think of latency as having three components. First, there is the speed-of-light propagation delay. … Second, there is the amount of time it takes to transmit a unit of data. This is a function of the network bandwidth and the size of the packet in which the data is carried. Third, there may be queuing delays inside the network.

P08 · Latency = Propagation + Transmit + Queue

Latency = Propagation + Transmit + Queue; Propagation = Distance / SpeedOfLight; Transmit = Size / Bandwidth — where Distance is the length of the wire over which the data will travel, SpeedOfLight is the effective speed of light over that wire, Size is the size of the packet, and Bandwidth is the bandwidth at which the packet is transmitted.
