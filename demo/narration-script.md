# Learning Pulse demo: narration script (about 3:26)

Timings are approximate and follow the scenes in `learning-pulse-demo.mp4`. Slides are held long enough to read each section at a relaxed pace.

---

**[0:00 · Problem slide]**
Teachers can see that a student got an answer wrong. What they rarely see is *why*.

A score doesn't show the reasoning.
A generic chatbot just hands over the answer and leaves no evidence behind.

Teachers need AI that helps students think, stays inside their lesson, and keeps the teacher in control.

**[0:09 · Solution slide]**
That's what gave us the idea for Learning Pulse. The teacher builds a lesson from their own reading and approves it. An AI tutor proposes a teaching action, and the server checks that action before anything runs. And everything the student wrote, and every bit of help they were shown, goes back to the teacher as evidence.

**[0:17 · Teacher workspace]**
Here's the teacher's workspace. The overview shows where students are struggling. Insights group possible reasoning concerns, with supporting *and* contrary evidence, so one wrong answer is never treated as a diagnosis.

**[0:33 · Lesson authoring]**
To make a lesson, the teacher starts from their own reading and writes learning objectives tied to exact passages. The AI then drafts questions, and a second, independent AI pass reviews them. But the teacher reviews every question before anything is approved.

**[0:57 · Tutoring sequence and assignment]**
The teacher also reviews what the tutor is allowed to do. Then they publish an immutable lesson version and activate it for the class.

**[1:17 · Student slide]**
Now on the student's side, students use one conversation box. They can ask for help or submit an answer. Help is never graded, and if the tutor can't tell which one a student means, it asks first. These next scenes are real sessions from earlier today, using live model calls.

**[1:23 · Alex's session]**
This is Alex. He's working on how packet size and cable length affect delay. He asks for help, and the AI proposes an explanation. The server validates that action, and the help is recorded as assistance.

Then Alex answers, and his answer contains a misconception: he thinks a longer cable increases transmission delay.

**[1:57 · Alex, page 2]**
Because Alex's answer showed a misconception, the AI generated a new diagnostic question, grounded in the teacher's reading. Alex answers it correctly. Since he had already been shown help, the record marks that as help-assisted, not independent.

**[2:17 · Decisions slide]**
Here's what the model actually decided. A help question led to an explanation. A wrong answer led to a new diagnostic question. A correct answer led to moving on. The AI proposes, and the application validates and executes.

**[2:28 · Jordan]**
Jordan answered the first question correctly on his own and no extra help is needed. Different student, different decision,

**[2:40 · Teacher evidence]**
Back to the teacher. The roster shows Alex's path next to Jordan's, with help shown and independent work marked separately. Opening Alex, the teacher sees his original answers, the evaluations, the help he was shown, and the uncertainty.

**[3:11 · Follow-up page]**
From this evidence, the teacher can prepare a follow-up activity.

**[3:17 · Closing slide]**

A score tells a teacher what happened. It doesn't tell them why.

Learning Pulse helps close that gap. It shows how students are thinking, where they're struggling, and what support they've received.

Because the goal isn't to replace teachers with AI. It's to give teachers the insight they need to help every student learn.
